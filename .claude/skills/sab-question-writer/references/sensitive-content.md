# Sensitive content, high stakes and other people's material

Read this when the topic is clinical, legal or safety-critical, involves distressing content, or the materials contain student data, test banks or exam items. The ground rules in SKILL.md are the short version; this file says how to apply them without lecturing the instructor and while still delivering a full game.

Contents: 1. High-stakes calculations and facts · 2. Distressing topics (safe messaging) · 3. Requests that would give harmful uplift · 4. Student data in materials · 5. Test banks, exams and copyrighted figures

## 1. High-stakes calculations and facts

Nursing, pharmacy, engineering, law and safety courses: students copy the worked method into practice, and a wrong number taught as correct can cause harm. The validator can't catch a valid-looking wrong number.

- **Compute every number in code**: answers, each distractor's error path ("forgetting to convert lb to kg gives 72.6"), and every number in an explanation. Apply the stated rounding rule exactly, and show unrounded intermediate values in the explanation if the course rounds only at the end.
- **State units and the rounding rule in every stem** with a non-integer answer ("Round to one decimal place"), and choose the tolerance deliberately: `0` when the rounding is being tested, a small tolerance when it isn't.
- **Give reference values in the stem** (dose ranges, daily maximums, product strengths, legal thresholds) rather than testing recall of them, so the file never acts as a reference and students practise the method. Never invent a maximum or threshold; if you aren't sure of a value, leave it out or flag it.
- **Use fictional, unidentifiable cases** ("a 4-year-old weighing 16 kg"), never real patients or clients.
- **Follow the course's conventions** (conversion factors such as 2.2 lb/kg, rounding by volume, the institution's abbreviations) and confirm them in the blueprint.
- **In the summary**, list every drug, value, threshold and its basis, ask the instructor to check them against their formulary, policy or jurisdiction, and say the game is practice, not clinical or legal guidance.

## 2. Distressing topics (safe messaging)

Suicide, self-harm, abuse, sexual violence, genocide, trauma, eating disorders: common in nursing, psychology, social work, education and history. Some students will have lived experience.

- **Follow safe-messaging guidance**: no method or lethality details, no "committed suicide" (use "died by suicide"), person-first and non-stigmatising language, no sensational or graphic detail. Stigmatising wording may appear only as an option that the explanation corrects.
- **Correct the myths** the course targets (asking directly about suicide does not increase risk; a sudden calm doesn't mean the risk has passed) and make them the distractors.
- **Wildcards for that module stay neutral or about practice and self-care** (supervision, documentation, a colleague's support), never jokes and never events that happen to patients or victims. Mishap rows are filtered by module like everything else, so give the module its own.
- **In the summary**, suggest a content note before play, a way to opt out, and local support resources (verify crisis lines for the instructor's country or region; don't guess).
- The module still gets a full, rigorous game: knowledge and judgement items, real misconceptions, proper milestones.

## 3. Requests that would give harmful uplift

The file is shared with students and often posted publicly, so its content must be safe to publish whatever the course. Decline items that give real-world capability for serious harm: synthesis routes or enhancement of weapons, toxins or dangerous pathogens; working exploit or malware code; lethal doses or methods of self-harm; instructions for evading safety controls. This holds even when the course is legitimate.

Most such LOs have a safe framing that teaches the same understanding: mechanism at the conceptual level, history and policy, detection and diagnosis, defence and safety practice, ethics and regulation. Offer that framing, briefly say why, and carry on. Don't lecture.

## 4. Student data in materials

Instructors sometimes paste a gradebook, roster or item analysis along with their notes.

- Say so in your first reply, kindly ("your notes include a grade roster with names and accommodations; I won't use any of it in the file"), and suggest the instructor not upload that version elsewhere.
- Use only aggregate information: which items or misconceptions were common. That is often the most valuable input for distractors.
- Keep every name, ID, email, grade, accommodation, health or conduct note out of the questions, explanations, image names and summary, including indirect references ("the mix-up most of you made on the midterm").
- If asked to write questions about or for named students, decline and offer an alternative that targets the same misconception for everyone.
- When results are sent somewhere, suggest student numbers or initials instead of full names if the institution requires it.

## 5. Test banks, exams and copyrighted figures

- **Publisher test banks and paid question banks** are licensed to the instructor, usually with a ban on posting them. Use them for scope, LOs and level, then write original items: new stems, new options, new numbers. Don't paraphrase item by item.
- **Live or upcoming exam items** don't belong in a practice game, which reveals every answer and explanation to every player, and a `.lock` file doesn't change that (every student gets the password). Write parallel items on the same LOs; concept overlap is fine and unavoidable, and the summary should claim "no reused items", not "no overlap".
- **Copyrighted figures** (textbook images, published plots): redraw the idea from synthetic data rather than reusing the image.
- **Instructor's own past questions** may be reused if the instructor wants, but still check them for cues and errors.
- Explain the issue once, in a sentence or two, then deliver a complete game. Ask how the file will be shared: on a public link the plain TSV is an answer key anyone can read.
